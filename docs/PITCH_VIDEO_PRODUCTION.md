### Who: 00:00–01:00

When water is uncertain, choosing one crop is only part of the decision. A farmer must also ask what can follow it, when the field will be free, and what the household needs across the coming seasons.

We are a team of three web developers working on RITU, a crop-rotation decision-support concept for farmers in Rajshahi's Barind region. Our starting question is practical: how can environmental evidence and a farmer's own constraints become a plan that the farmer can understand, choose and revise?

This is a working concept demo. The environmental values and crop-matching rules shown today are authored mocks. NASA data integration and local agronomic validation are planned work.

### Why: 01:00–02:00

FAO has documented drought and variable water availability in the Barind region, and described how changing crop timing and sequences can help farmers respond to climate uncertainty. That establishes a real planning problem; it does not establish that our example rotations are suitable for a particular farm.

The decision spans more than the next harvest. Soil and drainage matter. Irrigation may be limited. A household may need to keep rice in the rotation. Planting and harvest may require help in particular months.

RITU brings these questions into one journey. The farmer records the conditions first, sees passing suggestions, and chooses among them before building a calendar. The goal is an understandable plan, with visible reasons and room to explore changing constraints.

### What: 02:00–03:00

First, the farmer enters a location, field area, soil, drainage and irrigation access. Optional settings record household crop needs, priorities, unavailable-help months and previous crops.

The demo checks its authored crop rules and planting windows. Only passing suggestions can be selected. The farmer chooses crops for the three seasons; RITU assigns the dates and builds up to three distinct passing crop sequences. Occupied month slots cannot overlap, and unused slots appear as rest. If a required season has no passing window, the demo explains the blocker instead of forcing a calendar.

The farmer can compare the plans and open the reasons behind the checks. Saving a calendar creates a local snapshot for recording planting, harvest and notes. The crop visualization is a schematic aid, separate from the planning rules.

### How: 03:00–04:00

Plans can change when water access changes. RITU's scenario view recalculates the mock candidates as irrigation, priority or mock climate changes. A draft does not overwrite the saved setup; the farmer applies a compatible scenario deliberately.

For the evidence-backed version, we plan to integrate NASA IMERG precipitation estimates and NASA POWER temperature histories, together with reviewed local crop windows and farm information. POWER meteorology uses reanalysis; those histories are not a field measurement or a forecast of next season's weather.

The next step is collaboration with agricultural reviewers and farmers: validate the crop rules, test the Bangla workflow and evaluate the plans against local conditions. We have not yet measured water savings, yield changes or soil improvement. RITU's promise is to make multi-season choices clearer, explainable and adaptable.
