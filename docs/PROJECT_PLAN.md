## The final plan: one farm, three seasons, three rotation options  *Build a visual crop-rotation planner for one locality in Bangladesh.* It will compare a farm’s current rotation with two locally supported alternatives, using NASA environmental data, soil information, irrigation access, and household needs.  The project combines *a working comparison tool, a documented farming story, and a clear seasonal calendar*. Your latest README remains the baseline, with the solo schedule replaced by the team responsibilities below.  ## 1. What the app will do  ### Screen 1 — This farm  Enter or load the selected farm’s:  - Current crops and planting dates. - Soil texture and drainage, including “unknown.” - Irrigation availability. - Crops the household must retain. - Relevant labor constraints.  *Visual:* the existing crop sequence on a seasonal calendar, with constraints marked against the months.  ### Screen 2 — Three options  Compare:  1. The current rotation. 2. A source-supported alternative. 3. A second source-supported alternative.  Each option shows:  - Planting and harvest windows. - Historical dry-spell exposure during supported crop stages. - Heat exposure where crop-stage timing and thresholds are supported. - Conflicts with farm requirements. - Crop-family diversity, legume inclusion, and supported cover/fallow information.  *Visual:* three aligned timelines, allowing users to compare the same months directly.  ### Screen 3 — Why this option?  Explain each rotation in *Bangla and English*:  - Which documented checks it passes. - What conflicts with the farm’s constraints. - What information still needs confirmation. - How the farmer’s priorities affect the comparison.  Use clear templates for these explanations.  *The main interaction:* change one constraint—such as irrigation availability—and see the affected options and explanations update.  ## 2. How the comparison works  Use a transparent sequence:  1. *Check the crop calendar:* planting windows, durations, overlaps, and sequences crossing December–January. 2. *Check farm constraints:* required crops, soil conditions, and supported irrigation requirements. 3. *Assign a status:* feasible under the documented checks, infeasible with a reason, or needs confirmation. 4. *Compare environmental exposure and soil-management characteristics separately.* 5. *Explain the trade-offs according to the farmer’s preference.*  A known blocking constraint remains visible even if other information is missing. An unknown input never silently becomes a pass.  If plausible changes to thresholds or planting dates reverse the comparison, say that *the preference is sensitive to those assumptions*. Sensitivity checks are not statistical confidence intervals or proof of agricultural performance.  ## 3. “This planting window encountered a qualifying dry spell in X of N usable historical years.”
  That describes historical exposure. It does not predict next season’s drought or yield.  ## 4. The story and visuals  ### Story
